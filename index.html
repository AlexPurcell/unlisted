How it works

  1. 01

    Your browser geocodes the place

    The town name you type is resolved to a bounding box by Nominatim, OpenStreetMap’s own geocoder. Cached locally so the same search never asks twice.

  2. 02

    It queries OpenStreetMap directly

    An Overpass API query pulls every mapped shop, restaurant, trade, clinic, salon and office inside that box — with whatever contact tags the map holds for each one.

  3. 03

    Gaps get scored, not guessed

    Businesses are ranked by how reachable they are and how large the gap is. A missing website tag is reported as exactly that — a missing tag, worth one phone call to confirm.

What this does not claim

OpenStreetMap is community-maintained, so a business with no website tag has no website on the map. Most of the time that means no website at all, and sometimes it means nobody has added it yet. This tool never collapses that distinction — every row says which it is, and confirming takes one search or one call. A lead list that lies to you is worse than no list.

Questions

Is this legal to use for client outreach?

The data is OpenStreetMap, published as open data under the ODbL licence, and this tool attributes it as required. These are publicly listed business details, not personal data. Your outreach itself is still yours to get right — follow the anti-spam rules that apply where you and the business are (CAN-SPAM in the US, GDPR and PECR in the UK and EU).

Why is my town showing so few businesses?

Coverage depends on how well volunteers have mapped your area. Dense cities are mapped thoroughly; some rural areas are thin. If your town looks sparse, that is a gap in the map rather than a gap in the town — and adding businesses to OpenStreetMap is free.

Do you store my searches?

There is no server to store them on. This is a static page; the requests go from your browser straight to OpenStreetMap’s public APIs. Geocode results are cached in your own browser’s localStorage so repeat searches stay polite to their servers.

Can I run or modify it myself?

Yes — it is MIT licensed and has no build step, no dependencies and no keys. Three files. Clone it, open index.html, or drop the folder on any static host.
